# 99. Bibliography

*Books, texts, and voices that have accompanied this exploration.*

This bibliography is not intended to be exhaustive or academic. It brings together works explicitly mentioned in this book and others that came up in the readings and conversations from which the project grew. They have not all had the same influence, and mentioning them does not mean that I agree with every conclusion or that I have read each one in its entirety.

I have kept many titles in their original language to make them easier to identify. Like the rest of this book, the list is open to corrections and additions.

## Works particularly relevant to this book

- **Culadasa (John Yates), Matthew Immergut, and Jeremy Graves** (2015). *The Mind Illuminated: A Complete Meditation Guide Integrating Buddhist Wisdom and Brain Science*. Dharma Treasure Press. One of the fundamental influences on the conditioned-mind model developed here.
- **Leigh Brasington** (2015). *Right Concentration: A Practical Guide to the Jhānas*. Shambhala Publications. A practical reference for exploring the *jhānas* and their different interpretations.
- **Rob Burbea** (2014). *Seeing That Frees: Meditations on Emptiness and Dependent Arising*. Hermes Amāra Publications. An exploration of emptiness, dependent origination, and ways of looking at experience.
- **Daniel M. Ingram** (2018, second edition). *Mastering the Core Teachings of the Buddha: An Unusually Hardcore Dharma Book*. An important reference in our conversations about *vipassanā*, maps of progress, and awakening.
- **Javier García Campayo** (2020). *Vacuidad y no-dualidad: Meditaciones para deconstruir el yo*. Editorial Kairós. A contemporary perspective on deconstructing the self and on non-dual practices.
- **Erich Fromm** (1956). *The Art of Loving* (*El arte de amar*). An inspiration for thinking of love as an ability that can be learned and cultivated.
- **Francisco J. Varela, Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch** (1991). *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience*. MIT Press. A meeting point between cognitive science, lived experience, and contemplative traditions.
- **Śāntideva** (eighth century). *Bodhicaryāvatāra* (*The Way of the Bodhisattva* or *A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life*, depending on the translation). A classical reference for compassion and the bodhisattva ideal.

## Other works that appeared in our conversations

The following references also appeared in our conversations about meditation, Buddhism, and philosophy. I include them as a map of readings and discussions, **not as a claim to have read every work in full**.

- **Mahāsi Sayadaw**. *Manual of Insight*; *The Progress of Insight*; *Practical Insight Meditation*. Three references on *vipassanā*, observation, and the development of insight.
- **Sayadaw U Paṇḍita**. *In This Very Life: The Liberation Teachings of the Buddha*. A practical account of the Mahāsi tradition.
- **Buddhaghosa** (fifth century). *Visuddhimagga* (*The Path of Purification*). A classical treatise on ethics, concentration, and wisdom in Theravāda Buddhism.
- **Upatissa**. *Vimuttimagga* (*The Path of Freedom*). Another classical treatise on practice and liberation.
- **Ācariya Anuruddha**. *Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha* (*Compendium of Abhidhamma*). A traditional synthesis of Abhidhamma; we have also mentioned the English edition *A Comprehensive Manual of Abhidhamma*, edited by **Bhikkhu Bodhi**.
- **Ajahn Brahm**. *Mindfulness, Bliss, and Beyond: A Meditator’s Handbook*. An interpretation of deep concentration and the *jhānas*.
- **Shaila Catherine**. *Focused and Fearless: A Meditator’s Guide to States of Deep Joy, Calm, and Clarity*; *Wisdom Wide and Deep: A Practical Handbook for Mastering Jhāna and Vipassanā*. Two approaches to concentration training and contemplative investigation. The first book has been republished under the title *The Jhanas*.
- **Bhante Henepola Gunaratana**. *Beyond Mindfulness in Plain English*. An introduction to deeper levels of meditative concentration.
- **Pa-Auk Sayadaw**. *Knowing and Seeing*. An account of meditative training in his tradition.
- **Richard Shankman**. *The Experience of Samadhi: An In-Depth Exploration of Buddhist Meditation*. An examination of different interpretations of *samādhi*.
- **Ajahn Pasanno and Ajahn Amaro**. *The Island: An Anthology of the Buddha’s Teachings on Nibbāna*. A collection on *nibbāna* from traditional sources.

## Ancient Buddhist texts cited or discussed

The book refers to discourses from the **Pali Canon**, especially the *Dīgha Nikāya* (DN), *Majjhima Nikāya* (MN), *Saṃyutta Nikāya* (SN), and *Aṅguttara Nikāya* (AN) collections. Among those we have cited or discussed are:

- *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta* (SN 56.11): the Four Noble Truths.
- *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta* (SN 22.59): the absence of an independent self in the five aggregates.
- *Nibbedhika Sutta* (AN 6.63): intention and karma.
- *Sallatha Sutta* (SN 36.6): the image of the two arrows.
- *Āhāra Sutta* (SN 46.51): the conditions that nourish the hindrances and the factors of awakening.
- *Nibbānapañhā Sutta* (SN 38.1): nirvana and the ending of greed, aversion, and ignorance.
- *Saṃyojana Sutta* (AN 10.13): the fetters or bonds.
- *Alagaddūpama Sutta* (MN 22): the teaching as a raft that must eventually be released.
- *Ariyapariyesanā Sutta* (MN 26): the noble search and the transmission of the teaching.
- *Ānāpānasati Sutta* (MN 118): mindfulness of breathing.
- *Sāmaññaphala Sutta* (DN 2): the fruits of the contemplative life.
- *Itivuttaka* 44: the two dimensions of *nibbāna* described in the canon.

Different translations of these texts can be consulted at [SuttaCentral](https://suttacentral.net/). As with any ancient text, it is worth distinguishing between what the source says, translation choices, and our own interpretations.
